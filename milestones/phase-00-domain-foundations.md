## Learn First
1. Patient: receives healthcare services.
2. Provider: the clinician or organization delivering care: physician, hospital, imaging center, pharmacy, specialist, and so on.
3. Payer: the organization responsible for paying healthcare claims, usually an insurance company or patient.
4. Health Plan: the specific insurance product and set of benefits/rules under which the patient is covered.
5. Care coordinator: Helps organize care across providers, services, appointments, referrals, and insurance requirements.
6. Prior-authorization specialist: handles administrative work around obtaining payer approval before certain services are performed.
7. Electronic Health Record: contains the patient's clinical information.
8. Encounter: Represents an interaction between the patient and healthcare system, such as an office visit or hospital admission
9. Diagnosis: describes a condition identified for the patient, commonly represented using ICD-10-CM codes.
10. Procedure: describes a service performed or requested, frequently represented using CPT or HCPCS codes.
11. Coverage: answers whether the patient's insurance is active and what benefits apply.
12. Referral: It is typically an authorization or direction from one provider-often primary care-to another provider or specialist.
13. prior authorization, or PA: It is a payer's review process for determining whether a proposed healthcare service satisfies the health plan's coverage requirements before the service is provided
14. Claim: It normally occurs after care has been delivered and requests reimbursement from the payer.
15. Denial: The payer did not approve the request. Reasons can include insufficient documentation, failure to satisfy coverage criteria, an excluded service, incorrect information, or other policy requirements
16. Appeal: It is the process used to challenge an unfavorable decision.
17. Clinical documentation: It is the supporting evidence behind the request.
